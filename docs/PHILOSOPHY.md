# The Blocks philosophy

*Simple, yet everything possible.*

Blocks starts from one belief: people don't want to learn a program; they want to make their website. And programmers don't want to learn a framework; they want to write a component and move on. Everything in Blocks serves these two wishes, in that order.

## Three gestures

Everything you do in Blocks is one of them:

1. **Drag** a block onto the page.
2. **Click** it.
3. **Type** what you want it to say.

Nothing else is required. Shortcuts and the Structure view are there, but they stay out of the way until you need them. If a new feature doesn't fit into one of the three gestures, it doesn't go into the editor.

## One material: the block

A Blocks site is made of blocks, and only of blocks. Some are small (a button), some are big (a section with columns), and the big ones are made of small ones. There is nothing "else": no hidden settings, no plugins, no themes that rewrite the rules, no special pages. Once you understand one block, you understand them all.

That is also the answer to "that's not possible": it is, with a new block, or with a block inside another block. Never with one more option.

## Eight rules

1. **It works the first time.** A block dropped on the page looks good with no settings at all. Settings are for changing things, not for getting started.
2. **One way to do a thing.** A component is a file. A page is a file. There is one way to add a block, offered in two places: the palette and the "+" button.
3. **What you see is what the visitor gets.** The same code displays the page in the editor and on the site. There is no separate "preview mode": there is the page.
4. **Nothing breaks because of a mistake.** Every edit can be undone. A block with an error doesn't stop the page. Messages say what happened and what you can do about it.
5. **Human words.** The editor doesn't say "slot", "prop" or "render". It says "area", "setting" and "display". If a word needs explaining, it's the wrong word.
6. **Simple for people, simple in code.** A feature that asks the user to learn a new concept, or the programmer to learn a new tool, isn't simple, however useful it is.
7. **Everything possible, through composition.** When something is missing, the answer is a new block, or an area where you put other blocks. Never one more checkbox.
8. **No dependencies, no build, no magic.** Node.js and a browser. Files you can open and read. A site you can pick up and take with you.

## What "quality" means

Not "a lot". A little, done well: few colors, plenty of space, one family of shapes, icons drawn by the same hand, words chosen with care. A thing has quality when there is nothing left to take away.

## What "safe" means

Safety isn't a stage; it's a condition. Everything new comes with its own protections and its own tests, or it isn't done. Passwords are never stored in plain text. The editor runs only on your computer, never on the public server. Everything a visitor types is treated as text, never as code.

## How we know it works

- Someone who doesn't write code builds a real site on their own in 30 minutes, without help.
- A programmer writes a new block in 10 minutes, from the docs alone.
- A visitor never notices that the site was made with Blocks.

When one of these stops being true, something has become too complicated, and we simplify it.
